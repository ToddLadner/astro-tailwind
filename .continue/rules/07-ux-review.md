# UX Review Standards

- Start with the user problem and task, not visual preference.
- Separate observed evidence from assumptions.
- Identify the primary user, context, emotional state, and likely interruptions.
- Evaluate hierarchy, clarity, cognitive load, actionability, error prevention, and recovery.
- Distinguish a usability defect from a product-strategy disagreement.
- Prioritize findings by user impact and frequency, not by how easy they are to fix.
- Give concrete recommendations tied to the identified problem.
- Challenge unnecessary complexity, duplication, and dashboard-like information overload.
- For distressed or high-conflict users, favor calm language, predictable behavior, clear next actions, and minimal ambiguity.
- Do not recommend dark patterns, manipulative urgency, or engagement for its own sake.
